The Northgate Bridge reopened to walkers and cyclists on Wednesday morning, three weeks ahead of the schedule the city published in the spring, after a rebuild that gave the 1912 stone bridge a new deck, restored lamps and a wider path on the upstream side.

A small crowd waited at the east end before seven o'clock. Some had crossed the bridge daily for decades before it closed in March; others had only ever seen it fenced off. When the last barrier was lifted, a retired letter carrier named Olive Hartnett was the first across, pushing the bicycle she said she had ridden over the river "about ten thousand times."

"I counted the planks on the way," she said at the far side. "They're all there. That's more than I could say last winter."

City engineers had warned in 2024 that the deck was wearing faster than expected, and a survey the following year found water working into the stone of two of the three arches. The council voted in January to close the bridge for a full rebuild of everything above the arches, a decision that split the neighbourhoods on either bank for most of the year.

The project manager, Tomas Reyes, said the early finish came down to two things: a dry autumn, and a decision in June to have the new lamp brackets cast locally rather than shipped from the coast. "The foundry is eleven blocks from the river," he said. "When a casting was wrong, we walked over and talked about it. That saved us a month, easily."

The new deck is laid over a waterproof membrane that should keep the arches dry, and the path on the upstream side is about a foot wider than before. Lighting runs along the inside of the railings at ankle height so that it does not shine into the windows of the houses on Mill Street, which residents had asked for at a public meeting in April. Two benches have been added at the midpoint, where the river can be seen in both directions.

Not every original feature survived. Two of the twelve cast-iron lamp standards were too corroded to restore, and have been replaced by copies made from moulds of the best-preserved one. The worst of the originals is now in the lobby of the Riverside Historical Society, along with a paving stone carved with initials and the date 1946.

The rebuild cost $4.1 million, about $300,000 under budget, according to figures the public works department released on Tuesday. Most of the money came from a state grant for active travel; the city's share was paid from its capital reserve.

For now the bridge is open only to people on foot and on bicycles. Cars return in the spring, once the approach roads at both ends have been rebuilt, and the council will decide before then whether one of the two traffic lanes should become a lane for bicycles. A group of business owners on the west bank has argued for keeping both lanes for cars.

Marisol Okafor, who runs a bakery on Front Street at the west end, said she had been worried about trade while the bridge was closed. "It's already better," she said, standing at her door as cyclists passed. "We had more people this morning than any Wednesday since the summer. People on foot buy bread. I hope the bicycle lane stays when the cars come back."

Others were less sure. A delivery driver who asked not to be named said the detour through the Calder Street bridge would add fifteen minutes to his route in the afternoons until spring. The city has said it will retime the signals at Calder Street before the end of the year.

Children from the Northgate Elementary School walked across at mid-morning as part of a lesson on the history of the river crossings. Their teacher, Ruth Ansel, said the class had spent a month studying photographs of the bridge's construction, including one of the masons posed on the half-built centre arch in 1911. "They wanted to know if the new workers would get a photograph too," she said. "So we took one."

The bridge will be open to walkers and cyclists around the clock. The city asks cyclists to keep to walking pace at the midpoint, where the benches narrow the path, and says it will review the arrangement after the first month.
